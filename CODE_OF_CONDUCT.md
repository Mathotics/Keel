# Code of Conduct

This standard says how people are expected to behave when they take part in Keel, and what happens when they do not.

Keel is proprietary. Permission to copy or modify it comes from the copyright holder, as [LICENSE](LICENSE) says. Anyone can still report a problem or propose a change. This standard applies to that conversation the same as it applies to people who already have permission to contribute.

Using this codebase for AI training is strictly prohibited. No artificial intelligence system, machine-learning model, automated agent, or similar system may use this software, its source, documentation, data, or any associated file for model training, machine learning, fine-tuning, embeddings, retrieval, or any related purpose. An artificial intelligence system may not access this project unless the copyright holder has given that system explicit prior written permission.

## Scope

This standard applies in every Keel project space:

- GitHub issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and commit messages
- Comments on those threads
- Any other channel used to talk about Keel, when the conversation is about this project

It also applies when someone is speaking for the project, including in a public write-up or at an event.

A disagreement about the software belongs here. A private dispute that has nothing to do with Keel does not.

## Expected behavior

- Keep the conversation on Keel and on the change, bug, or question being discussed. A thread about a defect is for that defect. Take unrelated topics somewhere else.
- Be direct and specific. Say what is wrong, where it shows up, and what you think should change.
- Disagree about the change. Leave the person out of it.
- Read what the other person actually wrote before you answer.
- Accept review. A request for changes is part of the work, including when you are the one being asked to change something.
- Give people time to reply. A maintainer may decline a change, and that answer stands.
- Follow [CONTRIBUTING](.github/CONTRIBUTING.md) when you propose a change: one ticket, a branch named for it, and a pull request that says what changed and how you tested it.
- Keep another person's private information private. That includes names, addresses, email, and anything they did not put in the thread themselves.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Insults, name-calling, and personal attacks
- Trolling, baiting, and piling on
- Harassment, threats, intimidation, and stalking
- Publishing another person's private information without permission
- Sexual comments, sexual jokes aimed at a person, and sexual advances
- Spam, and using project spaces to campaign for something unrelated to Keel
- Continuing a line of argument after the copyright holder has closed it
- Encouraging or helping anyone else do any of the above

## Reporting

Report a violation to the copyright holder. Do not put the report in a public GitHub issue. A public thread is the wrong place for a complaint about a person, and it is the wrong place for private details.

Say what happened, when it happened, and where. Include links or copies of the messages. Say who was involved if you know. Say if you want the report kept quiet.

Reports are read by the copyright holder. He does not pass the report to the person it is about unless he has to in order to look into it, and he does not publish the reporter's private details.

A report made to punish someone, or a report the reporter knows is false, is itself a violation.

## Enforcement

The copyright holder looks into the report and decides what happens. He may act on a public violation without waiting for a report. He may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, issues, and other contributions that break this standard.

Consequences match the behavior:

1. **Correction.** A private or public note that names the problem and asks the person to stop. This is for a first or mild break, such as an insult in one comment.
2. **Warning.** A written warning that names the behavior and states that the next break leads to a block. The person is asked to stop contacting the reporter about the complaint.
3. **Temporary block.** The person is blocked from project spaces for a stated period. They may not interact with the people involved, in public or in private, about the project during that period. A pull request or issue they opened may be closed.
4. **Permanent block.** The person is blocked from project spaces for good, and any permission to contribute is taken back.

The copyright holder may skip steps when the behavior is serious. Threats, publishing private information, and sexual advances start past a correction.

Those decisions are final for this project.
